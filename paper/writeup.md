# The 16GB Ceiling

## Test-time training for ARC-AGI-2 on one consumer GPU, and what it takes to trust a number in this competition

**Submission ID 55261090, scored 32.64 on the hidden set. That is the maximum of seven runs of one immutable artifact whose mean is 30.44 and standard deviation 1.42. Most of this paper is about why we now report it that way.**

### What we built

Our submission reproduces the strongest public lineage in this competition, the ARChitects' 2024 pipeline as ported to ARC-AGI-2 by Ivan Sorokin, on his 4B grid model. Each task gets its own rank-256 LoRA adapter trained on augmented views of its demonstrations, a depth-first search enumerates every completion whose cumulative probability stays above 0.2, and a selector picks two attempts by vote count and mean augmentation likelihood. We credit the method fully. Our contribution is what the field does not usually publish about a method: the error bar, the controls, the theory of where it stops, and two audits of the numbers everyone is chasing.

The same pipeline prices the constraint in our title. Our best single-card configuration reaches 18.19 on a 16GB RTX 5070 Ti. The identical method on the contest's four L4s averages 30.44 over seven runs. Same base model, same hidden set, same twelve hours. One consumer card costs about twelve points, and that gap belongs to the per-task training, which none of the fourteen configurations we measured could serve at full rank and context inside 16GB.

Be clear about where that sits. It is mid-field, far below the leaders, and twenty-one entries share it exactly, which is what forking a public notebook produces. We built and measured thirty-three levers on top of it. None moved the hidden score, and eleven were closed with no GPU at all by pricing the idea before building it. The honest summary of our season is that the reproduction was the peak. What follows is what we could measure rigorously from there, and it says this leaderboard is noisier and more contaminated than its numbers suggest.

### The error bar nobody measures

Every comparison on this leaderboard silently assumes re-running a fixed configuration returns the same score. The 2025 winners mention one to two points of run-to-run variability in passing. Nobody, including them, measured it. We did, by spending seven submissions re-running one unchanged artifact, and it demolished our own conclusions. One Kaggle kernel version, verified identical by script version rather than by description, scored **30.56, 32.64, 28.47, 31.39, 30.56, 30.56 and 28.89**: a spread of 4.17 points, ten task credits, mean 30.44 and standard deviation **1.42**.

Measured against that, **every build we called a regression falls inside 1.8 standard deviations**, and so do both public notebooks we ran as controls and our own best configuration. We widened this estimate four times, always in the same direction, each widening retiring claims from earlier drafts. The mechanism is throughput: the search is deadline-bounded in three places, so how far the machine gets decides where it truncates and which candidates exist. The consequence is a budget, not a caveat. Separating two configurations at 80 percent power needs roughly 3.4 points and three runs per arm, so one submission a day permits about eight properly powered comparisons in a season. Our ledger of thirty-three nulls is therefore partly about the levers and partly about the instrument: single-submission A/B testing, which this field and we both ran, was never capable of the discrimination it claimed.

### Why it works, and exactly where it stops

The base model is bimodal per task. It either internalises the rule during test-time training, after which the search finds the answer with high probability, or it internalises a wrong one and concentrates its mass there. On our measured slice, 65 percent of tasks never produce the correct grid under any augmented view, and they consume 71 percent of the budget failing.

We prove a theorem about the half of the pipeline everyone tunes. Any selector ranking candidates on statistics derived from the adapted model's own likelihoods is bounded by the expected top-two posterior mass of its beliefs. The reading is Blackwell's: selection is a garbled channel, and no processing recovers what it never carried. On our dumped pools the truth, when it enters at all, is Pareto-dominated by wrong grids on multiplicity and likelihood together, so the bound bites. Refitting selector weights under leave-one-task-out validation scores worse than the deployed selector. Cell-wise voting reconstructs zero of the 41 missing truths, because the pool's errors are correlated through the model's beliefs.

The theorem names its own two escape routes, and we ran both. Executing task-side structure against the demonstrations, tried as a symbolic object-count prune, scored 27.92, inside the noise band, so the conclusion rests on the offline experiment instead. Mining hard invariants from each task's demonstrations, size maps, palettes and component counts, kills eleven of nineteen correct answers: with two or three demonstrations coincidental regularities are common, and ARC-AGI-2 test pairs are built to break exactly those. Pruning back to invariants that never kill a truth leaves only properties the adapted model already respects, so they kill no wrong candidate either. Selectivity and safety are the same axis. Replaying nine further proposed filters against a dumped pool found seven that false-kill and two that fire on nothing at all, because the model already satisfies them. An external oracle helps only if it beats the model's own beliefs about the same property; a noisy one is strictly worse than none. The second route, rescoring under a separately trained model, produced our strangest result, below.

### Two audits of the numbers this field runs on

A public notebook displayed 33.89 against our 30.56, and we spent a week producing three quantified explanations of the deficit. Then we forked it unmodified and submitted it: 28.06. The notebook actually titled for 33.89, forked byte-identical with its author's attachments, scored 28.47. Both land near 28 when executed, which our own pipeline also reaches on a bad day, so the two are indistinguishable. A displayed score binds to a submission, not to the published file, and both are mutable independently. The week was aimed at a number that does not exist. The remedy is running the unmodified fork as a control before explaining anything.

The second audit began when a public 2B checkpoint appeared to solve candidate selection perfectly, truth top-two on 19 of 19 reachable outputs. A perfect score against a ceiling is a symptom. That model greedy-decodes 80 percent of the public evaluation set exactly with no test-time training, while the 4B from the same author manages 10 percent. On 120 fresh RE-ARC instances generated under our own seed after both models were published, the ordering reverts: the 4B at 78 percent against the 2B's 65. The interaction is 83 points at eight standard errors. A smaller model does not beat a larger sibling eight-fold on harder published data while losing on easier fresh data. The public evaluation set is not a valid measurement instrument for that checkpoint, and any pipeline scoring or filtering against it is reading recall. We claim nothing about intent; training on the public split is standard when the private split is what scores.

These two results are one finding: the competition's visible numbers, displayed scores and public-eval accuracies, are not the measurements they appear to be, and both failure modes are detectable with cheap controls we provide.

### What transfers beyond ARC

The contamination test is a general instrument. Establish a capability ordering between models on data generated after they were published, measure the same models on the suspect benchmark, and read contamination off the interaction. It needs no training data, no private split and no author cooperation, only a task generator. The selection theorem applies to any propose-then-rerank pipeline, including most best-of-n LLM inference, and says when reranking cannot help and what an external verifier must beat first. The replicate control is the cheapest discipline any competitor can adopt, and it is how we caught our own error bar being too small, four times.

### What this does for anyone chasing a top score

Negative results with controls are directions not to dig. Selector reweighting, cell-wise voting, attempt diversification and best-first decode order are dead by measurement, the last for one logging run. The union of three independently trained adapters equals the best single seed, the apparent gain being decoder nondeterminism. Displayed public scores are not reproduction targets. One result earns its space: masking the training loss on cells a rule leaves unchanged, 88 percent of a median output, made the transformed cells 4.6 times more accurate and the answer worse, while an equal-size random mask was indistinguishable from baseline. The gradient spent on copying is the load-bearing half.
