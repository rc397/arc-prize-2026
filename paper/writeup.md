# The 16GB Ceiling

## Test-time training for ARC-AGI-2 on one consumer GPU, and what it takes to trust a number in this competition

**Submission ID 55261090, scored 32.64 on the hidden set.**

### What we built

Our submission is a reproduction of the strongest public lineage in this competition, the ARChitects' 2024 pipeline as ported to ARC-AGI-2 by Ivan Sorokin, on his 4B grid model. Each task gets its own rank-256 LoRA adapter trained on sixteen augmented views of the demonstrations, a depth-first search enumerates every completion whose cumulative probability stays above 0.2, and a selector picks two attempts by vote count and mean augmentation likelihood over fresh augmentations. We credit the method fully. Our contribution is everything the field does not usually publish about a method, the error bar, the controls, the theory of where it stops, and two audits of the numbers everyone else is chasing.

The same pipeline prices the constraint in our title. Our best single-card configuration reaches 18.19 on a 16GB RTX 5070 Ti. The identical method on the contest's four L4s averages 30.95 over five runs and peaks at 32.64. Same author, same base model, same hidden set, same twelve hours. One consumer card costs about twelve points, and that gap is a property of the per-task training, which cannot be served at full rank and context inside 16GB by any of the fourteen configurations we measured.

Be clear about where 32.64 sits. It is mid-field, far below the leaders, and twenty-one entries share it exactly because that is what forking a public notebook produces. Every method change we made scored at or below the mean of that configuration's own runs, and none scored above it. The honest summary of our season is that the reproduction was the peak and our own additions cost points. What follows is what we could measure rigorously from that position, and two of those measurements say this leaderboard is noisier and more contaminated than its numbers suggest.

### The error bar nobody measures

Every comparison on this leaderboard silently assumes that re-running a fixed configuration returns the same score. The 2025 winners mention one to two points of run-to-run variability in passing. Nobody, including them, measured it. We did, and it cost us our own first answer. Three runs of the unchanged pipeline scored 30.56, 30.97 and 30.56, spanning one task credit, and we wrote that down as the noise floor. Then we resubmitted a single immutable Kaggle kernel version, the identical committed artifact that had already scored 30.56. It scored 32.64. Two executions of the same code, 2.08 points apart, five task credits, with no confound available. Five runs of the method now span 2.64 points with a standard deviation of 1.01, so the floor is five credits and not one. Under the corrected bar our largest regressions survive near three sigma and our smallest does not: a result we had reported as costing points is an ordinary draw. The mechanism is throughput, since the search is deadline-bounded and session speed decides where it truncates, and that is precisely the explanation we had dismissed because three runs failed to spread.

### Why it works, and exactly where it stops

The base model is bimodal per task. It either internalises a task's rule during test-time training, and the search then finds the answer with high probability, or it internalises a wrong rule and concentrates its mass there. On our measured slice, 65 percent of tasks never produce the correct grid under any augmented view, and they consume 71 percent of the budget failing.

We prove a theorem about the half of the pipeline everyone tunes. Any selector that ranks candidates using only statistics derived from the adapted model's own likelihoods is bounded by the expected top-two posterior mass of the model's beliefs. The reading is Blackwell's, selection is a garbled channel and no processing recovers information the channel never carried. On our dumped candidate pools the truth, when it enters the pool at all, is Pareto-dominated by wrong grids on multiplicity and likelihood together, so the bound bites. Refitting selector weights under leave-one-task-out validation scores worse than the deployed selector, the headroom is real but not learnable from sixty outputs. Cell-wise voting across candidates reconstructs zero of the 41 missing truths, because the pool's errors are correlated through the model's beliefs.

The theorem names its own two escape routes, and we ran both. Executing task-side structure against the demonstrations, tried as a symbolic object-count prune, scored 27.92, three standard deviations below the mean of the unchanged configuration's five runs. An offline test of the same route agrees and explains it. Mining hard invariants from each task's own demonstrations, size maps, palettes and component counts, kills eleven of nineteen correct answers, because with two or three demonstrations coincidental regularities are common and ARC-AGI-2 test pairs are built to break exactly those. Pruning back to the invariants that never kill a truth leaves only properties the adapted model already respects, so they kill no wrong candidate either. Selectivity and safety are the same axis. The escape clause therefore has a measured precondition, an external oracle helps only if it is more accurate than the model's own beliefs about the same property, and a noisy oracle is strictly worse than none. The second route, rescoring under a separately trained model, produced our strangest result, below.

### Two audits of the numbers this field runs on

A public notebook displayed 33.89 against our 30.56, and we spent a week explaining the deficit, with three quantified explanations. Then we forked the notebook unmodified and submitted it. It scored 28.06. The notebook actually titled for 33.89, forked byte-identical with its author's own attachments, scored 28.47. Two public artifacts displaying 33.89-class numbers both land near 28 when executed, below our own unchanged pipeline's 30.56 to 30.97 band. A displayed score binds to a submission, not to the published file, and both are mutable independently. The week of explanations was aimed at a number that does not exist. The remedy is not scepticism, which produced its own wrong conclusion, but running the unmodified fork as a control before explaining anything.

The second audit began when a public 2B checkpoint appeared to solve candidate selection perfectly, placing the truth top-two on 19 of 19 reachable outputs. A perfect score against a ceiling is a symptom. That model assigns the true public evaluation answers a median of 0.0002 nats per token and greedy-decodes 80 percent of them exactly with no test-time training, while the 4B from the same author manages 10 percent. On 120 fresh RE-ARC instances we generated under our own seed after both models were published, each verified by RE-ARC's own verifier, the ordering reverts, the 4B at 78 percent against the 2B's 65. The interaction is 83 points at eight standard errors. A smaller model does not beat a larger sibling eight-fold on harder published data while losing on easier fresh data. The public evaluation set is not a valid measurement instrument for that checkpoint, and any pipeline using it to score or filter against public eval is reading recall. We claim nothing about intent, training on the public split is standard practice when the private split is what scores.

These two results are one finding. The competition's visible numbers, displayed leaderboard scores and public evaluation accuracies, are not the measurements they appear to be, and both failure modes are detectable with cheap controls that we provide.

### What transfers beyond ARC

The contamination test is a general instrument. Establish a capability ordering between models on data generated after they were published, measure the same models on the suspect benchmark, and read contamination off the interaction. It needs no training data, no private split, no author cooperation, only a task generator. The selection theorem applies to any propose-then-rerank pipeline, which includes most best-of-n LLM inference, and says when reranking cannot help and what an external verifier must beat before it does. The replicate control, spending one submission to re-run an unchanged configuration, is the cheapest discipline any competitor can adopt, and it is how we caught our own first error bar being too small.

### What this does for anyone chasing a top score

Negative results with controls are directions not to dig. Colour-permutation restarts do not decorrelate misses, the union of three independently trained adapters equals the best single seed, the apparent gain being decoder nondeterminism. Selector reweighting, cell-wise voting, attempt diversification and best-first decode order are dead by measurement, the last for the price of one logging run. Noisy symbolic pruning costs six credits. Displayed public scores are not reproduction targets. And the twelve-point price of one consumer card tells solo entrants where the method ceiling actually is before any engineering starts.
